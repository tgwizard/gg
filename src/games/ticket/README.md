# Coast to Coast

How it's built, for working on it: the platform it sits on (rooms, the game contract, the
kit, bots) is in [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](../../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

Ticket to Ride's rules on its North American map (and maps of our own), under our
own name: fan-made, not affiliated with Days of Wonder, and credited as such in its
tagline, setup and rules. The art (maps, cards, icon) is our own.

The standard game for 2–5 players: 110 train cards (12 of each of eight colors, 14
locomotives), five face up (three locomotives showing and all five are replaced), on
one of several maps.
- **Maps** (`maps/`, listed in `maps/index.ts`) are data, built with `defineMap` from
  `map.ts`: cities with three-letter codes, routes with ids like `SEA-HEL`
  (`DUL-OMA/1`, `/2` for double routes), lengths and colors, tickets, and how many
  trains each player gets. The game's config names its map (`TtrConfig.map`; a game
  from before there were several has none, and is on North America), and so does its
  view (`config.map`), so the engine, bot and UI all look things up in the game's own
  map. `graph.ts` answers questions about a map: whether a route is open to a player
  (double-route rules), whether routes connect two cities, the longest continuous
  path, and the cheapest way to finish a ticket (for the built-in bot).
  - *North America* (`usa`): the standard board, 36 cities, 100 routes, 30 tickets, 45
    trains each, checked against a published route list.
  - *Stockholm* (`stockholm`, our own, being made): the inner city's railways
    (tunnelbana, pendeltåg, Tvärbanan, Saltsjöbanan, Lidingöbanan, Roslagsbanan, Spårväg
    City) after SL's rail network map, most stops left out, with some ferries and a few
    links we imagined. It runs from Bromma to Lidingö and Solna to Skarpnäck; places
    farther out (Arlanda, Täby, Vaxholm, Saltsjöbaden, Nynäshamn, Södertälje, Hässelby,
    Kista) sit on its edge, toward where they are. 47 stations, 87 routes (11 ferries),
    38 tickets, 40 trains each.
  - A map's `status` says how far along it is: `dev` maps are only offered when running
    locally (`offeredMaps`, in the table's setup), `wip` ones to everyone with a WIP
    tag, `ready` ones plainly. Commit a new map as `dev` and move it on from there.
- **Ferries** (as in Ticket to Ride: Europe): a gray route with `ferry` set takes at
  least that many locomotives, the rest in any one color. On the board its
  locomotive spaces are the locomotive card's rainbow with a little engine on.
- **A turn** has stages: `start`, `second` (one card drawn, one to go) and `tickets`
  (choosing drawn tickets). Choosing the first tickets is simultaneous (`keep` is a
  `concurrentOp`). A claim names a color and a number of locomotives
  (`{route, color, locos}`; color `"loco"` for locomotives only); `legalOps` lists the
  fewest-locomotive way per color, the UI every way.
- **Hidden information:** hands, kept and offered tickets, cards drawn blind, the
  decks. Card, ticket and train counts are public, and so are face-up draws and the
  cards paid for a route. Tickets are revealed in the final score.
- **The end:** finishing a turn with 2 or fewer trains starts the last round, kept
  as the list of players still to play (`finalTurns`), so leaving or being skipped
  can't stall it. A player who can't do anything at all (no cards to draw, no route
  they can pay for, no tickets) is passed over; if nobody can, the game ends.
- **Away players** work as in Finns i sjön: running out of time draws two cards from
  the deck for you (or whatever's possible) and marks you away. Leaving mid-game
  discards your cards and returns your tickets; your trains stay on the map.
- **Boards** (`ui/boards/`): how each map is drawn, its viewbox, land and water, props
  (mountains, trees, waves, a compass) and any city names placed by hand; names not
  given are placed by `labelsFor` (`ui/geometry.ts`) to keep clear of tracks.
- **North America's geography** (`ui/boards/usa-geography.ts`) is generated by
  `node --import ./tools/ts-hooks.mjs tools/ttr-geography.ts` from
  Natural Earth's 1:50m countries, lakes and borders (public domain). The board
  isn't a real projection, so the outlines are bent onto it with a thin-plate spline
  fitted to the 36 cities' real coordinates (every city lands within a few pixels of
  its board position, so coastal cities stay on their coasts), then clipped and
  simplified to ~28 KB of SVG paths.
- **Stockholm's geography** (`ui/boards/stockholm-geography.ts`) is generated by
  `node --import ./tools/ts-hooks.mjs tools/ttr-stockholm-geography.ts <dir>` from
  OpenStreetMap's coastline and lakes (fetched from the Overpass API; the queries are
  in the tool), © OpenStreetMap contributors under the ODbL, credited on the board.
  The board is the inner city through a gentle fisheye (logarithmic in the distance
  from Gamla stan), so the middle of town is magnified a little; stations start where
  the fisheye puts them, and a thin-plate spline bends the shores to follow any station
  moved from there. The stations on the board's edge aren't in the tool's list of real
  positions, so the shores aren't bent for them. Rerun it after moving stations.
- The map is drawn as SVG (`ui/TicketMap.tsx`, `ui/geometry.ts`) in two layers: the
  printed board (coasts, lakes, borders, relief shading, paper texture), drawn once
  in its own layer so the costly filters aren't redrawn on hover, and the live
  tracks and cities on top. Train cars are spaced along straight or bowed tracks,
  curved where the printed board curves them, with label offsets per city. Train
  colors and the board's land and water are tokens (`--train-*`, `--map-*`).

- **Zooming** (`ui/ZoomableBoard.tsx`): pinch with two fingers (one still scrolls the page, or pans a
  zoomed map), + and −, or a trackpad pinch (ctrl + wheel), from the whole map (1×) to 3×, about the
  point under your fingers. During a pinch the map is only scaled (a CSS transform); when the fingers
  lift it's drawn at its new size, so it stays sharp without redrawing the shading every frame. It starts
  at the whole map, and the board keeps the map's shape and size at any zoom (a zoomed map moves inside
  it). To try pinching in Chrome: DevTools, the device
  toolbar (⌘⇧M), reload, then Shift + drag on the map.

- **The built-in bot** keeps its cheapest tickets and claims routes on the cheapest path to
  them.

## QA

Manual checks for after a change to this game (how to run them: [docs/QA.md](../../../docs/QA.md)).

- [ ] Two to five sit down; *Start* deals 4 cards and offers 3 tickets to each. Hovering a ticket points out its cities on the map; *Keep* needs at least 2. Others only see "choosing tickets".
- [ ] Draw two cards: face up or from the deck. A face-up locomotive ends the draw and can't be the second card. Three locomotives face up are replaced (the log says so). Only you see what you drew blind ("From the deck last turn", at the bottom of the tray). A card you took doesn't stay raised or ringed afterwards.
- [ ] The news under the map keeps its height whatever happened: whose move it is (and the last round), then the latest move, at most two lines. On your move it and your tray get the gold ring, flashing red in the last 10 s (not while you're away). Pressing it opens *What happened* (full width, newest first, *Load more* for older) and scrolls there. The tray's hint sits at its bottom and doesn't make it change height.
- [ ] On your turn, routes you can pay for are outlined; clicking one opens a bubble by the route (the rest of the board dims) with every way to pay (color, with or without locomotives); Cancel, Escape or a click elsewhere on the map backs out. Claimed routes show the player's trains (taller, with windows), distinct from the printed board in both modes.
- [ ] Double routes: with 2–3 players, claiming one track fades the other; with 4–5 the other stays open, but not to you.
- [ ] *Tickets* draws 3; keep at least 1. Completed tickets turn green with a ✓.
- [ ] Get someone to 2 or fewer trains: "last round" in chat and on the plates; each player gets one more turn, then the score table shows routes, tickets (each ✓/✗), the longest railway bonus and totals.
- [ ] Let the clock run out: two cards are drawn for you and you're marked away (dashed plate, "away" for others; yours says "Away: tap to come back", and the tray says so at the top); *I'm back* or tapping your plate gives you the rest of your turn. The first tickets' clock (twice as long) keeps the cheapest two.
- [ ] Hovering a player's nameplate fades everything but their trains; hovering a ticket joins its two cities with a dotted line.
- [ ] Cards you take fly from the face-up row or the deck to your hand; cards someone else takes fly from where they were to their line in the news, as backs from the deck, and the card there appears as it lands (none of it with reduced motion). The news and the log show the cards taken (face up in color, blind ones as backs, yours in color) and the cards paid for routes.
- [ ] A claimed route (anyone's) is laid down like a heavy chain from its first city: car by car, each swinging in from above and landing with a squash and a puff, in well under a second for six cars, then its glow. Nothing replays on reload; with reduced motion the trains are simply there.
- [ ] Three layouts (`useLayout`, by the game's width and the window's shape): on a laptop screen (1440×900, chat open) your side goes beside the map and the players, the map, the market, your cards and your tickets all fit without scrolling; a narrower or taller window (1280×800, or ~1100×1150 with chat folded) has the map the whole width with the news and tray beside your tickets under it; a phone has one column.
- [ ] Zoom: pinching the map zooms about your fingers (the page itself doesn't zoom), one finger scrolls the page (or pans a zoomed map), + and − step it (− greyed at the whole map, + at 3×), and claiming a route works zoomed in. On a laptop, a trackpad pinch zooms about the pointer.
- [ ] Maps: opening the table offers North America and, locally, Stockholm (tagged DEV; not offered in production while it's `dev`). The settings say which map a game is on; the rules' points table counts that map's routes.
- [ ] Ferries (Stockholm): their locomotive spaces show the rainbow and an engine; claiming one offers only ways with at least that many locomotives.
- [ ] Phone (360 and 412 wide): the players are compact cards two to a row, nothing spilling out; the map scrolls sideways; the market, your cards and tickets stay usable; the deck lines up with the face-up cards. The final scores fit in their card (no columns cut off, ticket lines wrapping per ticket).
